Supermassive Black Holes

Regions of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing—no particles or even electromagnetic radiation such as light—can escape. At the center of almost every large galaxy lies a supermassive black hole, millions to billions of times the mass of our sun. The boundary of no return is known as the Event Horizon.

Escape Velocity> Speed of Light
Famous ExampleSagittarius A*
💥

Neutron Stars & Pulsars

The collapsed core of a massive supergiant star that has exploded as a supernova. They are the densest and smallest stars known to exist. A mere teaspoon of neutron star material weighs about 4 billion tons. Pulsars are highly magnetized, rotating neutron stars that emit beams of electromagnetic radiation.

Density10^17 kg/m³
Rotation SpeedUp to 716 Hz
🌌

Stellar Nurseries (Nebulae)

Massive clouds of dust, hydrogen, helium, and other ionized gases. They act as nurseries for new stars. As regions of the nebula collapse under their own gravity, temperatures rise, eventually triggering nuclear fusion and the birth of a new star system.
